The formulas

Every number on this page comes from the thin-lens model. Distances are measured from the camera, lengths in millimetres unless marked.

Field of view

angle = 2 · atan( sensor size ÷ (2 · f) ) · across: width, up: height, corner to corner: diagonal

35 mm equivalent

crop = 43.27 ÷ sensor diagonal   equivalent = f · crop · 43.27 mm is the diagonal of 36 × 24

Circle of confusion

c = 0.030 mm ÷ crop · 0.030 on full frame, about 0.019 on APS-C, 0.015 on Micro Four Thirds: the same blur on the final picture

Hyperfocal distance

H = f² ÷ (N · c) + f · focus here and everything from H/2 to infinity is sharp

Near and far limit, focused at s

near = s (H − f) ÷ (H + s − 2f)   far = s (H − f) ÷ (H − s) · far is infinite once s ≥ H

Blur of a point at distance x

b = (f ÷ N) · f · |s − x| ÷ ( x (s − f) ) · on the sensor; sharp while b ≤ c

Check it yourself
50 mm on full frame sees 39.6° across and 46.8° corner to corner. At f/2.8, focused at 3 m, with c = 0.030 mm: sharp from 2.73 m to 3.33 m, 0.60 m of depth of field; the hyperfocal distance is 29.8 m.
How the picture blurs
Each object is blurred by its own b, worked out from its distance. The blur is a soft Gaussian of the same spread as the blur circle, so it reads a little softer than real bokeh, but grows and shrinks exactly as the formula says.
Why c changes with the sensor
A small sensor’s picture is enlarged more to reach the same screen, so its blur must be smaller on the sensor to look the same. Some calculators use the diagonal ÷ 1500 (0.029 mm on full frame) instead; the limits move by a few centimetres.
