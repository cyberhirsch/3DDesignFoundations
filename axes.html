Axes and units

Every program agrees that a chair has a top, a front and a size. They disagree about which axis points up, which hand the axes follow and what one unit means. See the conventions side by side, send a chair from one program to another, and find the setting that brings it in upright, unmirrored and at its real size.

Which way is up? · Weeks 1 and 10

Nine conventions, all drawn from the same point of view, so the same physical direction points the same way in every picture. Up is always up on screen: what changes is which letter it gets.

−40°
Right-handed

Curl the fingers of your right hand from X to Y: your thumb points along Z. Blender, Maya, Houdini, 3ds Max, glTF, USD and FBX.

Left-handed

The same with your left hand. Unreal and Unity. Going between the two mirrors one axis: Unreal’s importer flips Y, so Blender’s +Y becomes Unreal’s −Y.

Front means the side a model shows in that program’s front view. Blender’s front view looks along +Y, so a model faces −Y; its exporters call this “Y Forward, Z Up”. Unreal and Unity name a forward axis instead, the way an actor or a camera moves. USD stores only the up axis and the unit, no front.

    Pivot and transforms · before you export

    The pivot is the point an object moves, turns and scales around. Maya, Houdini, Unreal and USD call it the pivot; Blender calls it the origin. Put it where the object touches the floor: then the chair lands at height zero and turns on the spot instead of swinging around.

    Where is the pivot?

    Apply rotation and scale

    In Blender: select the object, Ctrl A › Rotation & Scale. Rotation becomes 0 and scale 1, and the mesh keeps its shape and size. Do it with a reason, after saving a version.

    • Why before export: exporters, engines, physics and modifiers read object scale differently. A scale of 0.01 or 100 travels as a number someone can drop; a mesh that really is 95 cm can’t lose its size.
    • Negative scale mirrors: it turns the faces inside out, and a game engine hides them. Apply it, then check Face Orientation: blue outside.
    • Leave location alone: applying location moves the pivot to the world origin. A prop keeps its pivot at its own base.
    • FBX adds its own: with its defaults, Blender’s FBX export writes every object with Rotation X −90 and Scale 100, to fit Y up and centimetres (checked in Blender 5.2). Unreal’s importer undoes it. For a clean file elsewhere: Apply Scalings › FBX Units Scale, and Apply Transform.

    Real size, in metres

    Blender counts in metres, Unreal in centimetres. Measure one object you know after every import. Ours, from the course kitchen:

    Chair, overall height0.95 m · 95 cm
    Chair, seat height0.45 m · 45 cm
    Table height0.76 m · 76 cm
    Boombox, width (Week 1)0.72 m · 72 cm
    Kitchen, wall to wall4.2 m · 420 cm
    A person, for scale1.80 m · 180 cm

    Wrong by a few centimetres: the model. Wrong by 100 times: a unit. Fix it at the source, in Blender, and export again. Scaling it in Unreal hides the mistake until the next import.
